WHAT IT DOES

A high-frequency sensorless field-oriented control (FOC) drive engine optimized for low-inductance UAV motors. Runs a rigid 24 kHz control loop on an STM32G431 with a State-Space Flux Observer, hardware CORDIC acceleration, predictive phase advance, and D-axis feedforward — all inside a 50 µs hard-sync budget.

Telemetry: 49-byte binary struct streamed at 1 kHz over a 2 Mbaud UART, with a custom PyQtGraph GUI visualizing Id, Iq, Vd, Vq, and phase-lag angle in real time.

WHY IT'S PUBLIC

The repository doubles as an engineering post-mortem — documenting not just what works, but why standard textbook industrial algorithms fail under microhenry, ultra-low-mass operating conditions. Failure modes shared so the FOC community can avoid them.

THE FAILURE MODE

At 40–50% throttle, a 40 µs computational latency combined with reactive back-EMF cross-coupling (V = −ω · L · Iq) forces massive D-axis current spikes that derail the flux observer. The motor crashes into "the jerk" — a non-linear instability boundary unique to microhenry, ultra-low-mass environments.

TEXTBOOK FAILURES DOCUMENTED

  • ADC slew limiting — clamping di/dt starved the observer. A 31 µH coil at 12 V naturally generates ~387,000 A/s rise rate; typical hardware anti-aliasing filters physically cannot pass this, so the observer reads a clipped current waveform.
  • Digital flywheel PLLs — inertial filters introduced excessive mathematical phase lag, failing to track the sharp mechanical acceleration of light drone props. The estimator converges to a "smooth" wrong answer.
  • 48 kHz PWM / 24 kHz FOC — gate driver deadtime (~0.5 µs) physically swallows a significant fraction of low-throttle pulses (2.08 µs wide at 10% throttle), starving the stator of the voltage the observer expects. Result: observer-model divergence.

THE FIX

Reverted to a strictly synchronous 1:1 execution framework — 24 kHz PWM / 24 kHz FOC — preserving physical pulse widths. Added predictive phase advance (aiming voltage where the rotor will be, ~40 µs forward), D-axis feedforward (−ω · L · Iq) to cancel cross-coupling, and an alpha-beta tracking filter as a "digital flywheel" (predictive momentum instead of lag-inducing low-pass).

POWER STAGE

  • Discrete IR2101 gate drivers · AOT418 MOSFETs
  • 6N137 optocoupler isolation between logic and power stage
  • 2 mΩ low-side shunts + AD8418 current sense amplifiers
  • MP1584 buck converter (12 V → 5 V)

ANALOG FRONT END

  • BEMF RC filter: 172.1 kHz cutoff → deterministic 7.9° phase lag, compensated in firmware
  • Injected ADC sampling aligned to PWM center-count events (quiet zone) to read clean phase currents
  • Hardware deadtime physically characterized at 900 ns

5-STAGE SPOOLING SEQUENCE

// OFF → CALIBRATE → ALIGN → RAMP → BLEND → CLOSED_LOOP_FOC

             FOC_BLEND, CLOSED_LOOP_FOC };

PREDICTIVE PHASE ADVANCE

Compensates the ~40 µs CPU + PWM hardware delay by aiming voltage where the rotor will be:

theta_cmd = theta_est + omega_est * 40e-6f;

D-AXIS FEEDFORWARD

Dynamic cross-coupling cancellation, mapped directly into the Inverse Park Transform:

Vd_ff = -omega_est * Ls * Iq;

TRACE TIMING

Core control loop executes in exactly 2,728 clock cycles at the current clock speed: 16.05 µs total — well within the 50 µs maximum budget of the 24 kHz loop.
