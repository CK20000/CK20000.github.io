Vision-Based PCV Analyzer — Final Year Research
2021 – 2025 · University of Moratuwa · Best Paper MERCon 2026
MAE:1.535 pp CLIA:78% within speedup:45–47% rotor:5000 RPM lidless safety factor:6.6 patent:NIPO 23851

WHAT IT DOES

A fully integrated instrument that replaces manual microhematocrit reading with a deep-learning segmentation pipeline. A lidless, passively-retaining centrifuge rotor enables simultaneous multi-tube imaging; a U-Net with a ResNet-18 encoder computes per-tube hematocrit directly from RBC/plasma masks.

The instrument returns per-tube HCT values to a mobile application without requiring the operator to visually identify the RBC–plasma boundary — the single largest source of reading error in conventional PCV testing.

CLINICAL RESULT

Validated against a clinical hematocrit analyzer on 50 independent capillary samples: 1.535 pp mean absolute error (vs 2.202 pp for manual reading), 78% of predictions within CLIA proficiency tolerance (vs 46% manual), and 45–47% reduction in total processing time — with no throughput penalty.

THE PROBLEM

Manual HCT reading introduces 2–5% reading error and substantial inter-operator variability — even trained students make reading errors, and the protocol requires each tube to be individually aligned against a graduated card. Conventional rotors use opaque lids that obstruct optical access and require manual handling, which frequently remixes the buffy coat and further degrades diagnostic accuracy.

THE HARD PART

The measurement itself is geometrically trivial — HCT is just a ratio of column heights. The engineering challenge was making the reading automatic without sacrificing the physical preconditions that make an accurate reading possible: unobstructed optics, structural stability at 5000 RPM, and vibration isolation low enough that the RBC–plasma boundary isn't disturbed.

THE FIX — THREE COUPLED SUBSYSTEMS

  • Lidless passive retention. Centrifugal force itself wedges each capillary tube into a profiled mechanical ledge, achieving a self-locking interference fit without any active clamping. A Rule-of-Three analysis over 50 high-speed cycles with zero ejections gives an upper failure-probability bound of p ≤ 6% at 95% confidence.
  • Passive vibration isolation. A four-stack rubber/foam isolator, sized against a Monte Carlo worst-case unbalance force of 11.78 N (99th percentile), delivers a 3.2× RMS acceleration reduction and a 10× cumulative-energy reduction at the rotor–motor interface.
  • Deep-learning segmentation. A U-Net with a ResNet-18 encoder segments RBC and plasma regions pixel-wise. Training is stabilized against severe class imbalance using a combined cross-entropy + Dice loss (0.5 / 0.5).

ROTOR DESIGN

  • FDM-printed PLA monolithic disk, lidless architecture
  • Passive retention ledge — profiled mechanical constraint creating an interference fit
  • Transition ramp guiding the tube's two-stage seating motion (lateral, then radial)
  • Clear optical window above each tube slot, compatible with a top-down CMOS sensor at > 50 mm working distance
  • Distal vent holes to relieve asymmetric aerodynamic pressure gradients at speed

STRUCTURAL VALIDATION (5000 RPM)

  • Peak Von Mises stress at retention ledge: 3.74 MPa vs 25.0 MPa derated PLA yield
  • Overall safety factor 6.6 against the combined yield/fracture criterion
  • First natural frequency: 124.88 Hz → sub-critical ratio λ = 1.50 (above the 1.2 threshold)
  • Operational speed is set at 5000 RPM because higher speeds trigger clay sealant blowout, not rotor failure

VIBRATION ISOLATION

Four identical square-cross-section stacks of alternating rubber and closed-cell foam, mounted symmetrically beneath the motor mount. Sized against a Monte Carlo worst-case unbalance force that peaks at partial loading (~12 tubes), not at full load — a non-obvious result of mass self-averaging.

  • RMS acceleration: 3.2× reduction (0.218 g → 0.068 g)
  • Peak acceleration: 3.4× reduction
  • Cumulative vibration energy ∫a²dt: 10.0× reduction

WHY THIS ARCHITECTURE

  • Object detectors ruled out. Up to 24 densely packed, overlapping tubes per image degrade detection feature-map resolution and cause NMS conflicts. Pixel-wise segmentation is required because HCT is fundamentally a boundary-position measurement.
  • Vision transformers ruled out. ViTs require large-scale pretraining to match CNN performance; the 411-image dataset is not sufficient to pretrain from scratch.
  • Class-weighted cross-entropy + Dice. Naïve cross-entropy converges poorly under 99.6% background. Dice directly optimizes region overlap, complementing the weighted CE.

POST-PROCESSING

Predicted masks are converted to per-tube HCT values through connected-component analysis. RBC and plasma regions are extracted as separate components, small isolated regions are discarded, and each RBC component is paired with its nearest plasma component by centroid distance. Each rotor slot carries a printed identifier extracted by OCR, associating each measurement with its sample record.

WHAT'S NEXT

The instrument's demonstrated accuracy is currently validated under one fixed illumination configuration and one rotor design. Extending to multiple devices, lighting conditions, and sealant colors is the primary direction for further work.
